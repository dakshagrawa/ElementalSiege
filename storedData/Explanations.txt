Topic: Atomic Structure
Questions: 1, 2, 4, 9
Everything in chemistry is made of atoms, so this is where every chemist begins.
Atoms contain 3 main subatomic particles:
Protons → positive charge (+)
Neutrons → no charge (0)
Electrons → negative charge (-)
The number of protons is extremely important because it determines what element the atom is.
For example:
1 proton = Hydrogen
6 protons = Carbon
8 protons = Oxygen
The atomic number ALWAYS equals the number of protons and is the main identification for elements.
The mass number tells you the total particles in the nucleus:
	Mass number = protons + neutrons.
How to Solve Questions
Need atomic number? → count protons
Need mass number? → add protons + neutrons
Negative particle? → electron
Determines element identity? → proton

Topic: Chemical Bonding

Atoms bond together to become more stable.

Covalent Bonds
atoms SHARE electrons
usually between nonmetals (right side of periodic table)
Example:
H₂O
CO₂
Ionic Bonds
electrons are TRANSFERRED
usually between a metal and a non-metal

Topic: Law of Conservation of Mass
Questions: 12, 63
The Law of Conservation of Mass states:
Matter cannot be created or destroyed.
During chemical reactions, atoms only rearrange. There is NO change in mass – the atoms we started with are also the atoms we end with, but in different structures.
Even when substances change form, the amount of matter remains constant.
How to Solve Questions
If the question mentions:
matter not being created or destroyed
→ choose Law of Conservation of Mass



Topic: Molar Mass
Questions: 13, 56
Molar mass tells you the mass of one mole of a substance. A mole is a unit of volume and one mole of substance is measured as 6.022 x 10^23 particles. Molar mass tells you how much 6.022 x 10^23 particles of a substance weigh in grams.
To calculate molar mass:
Find the element’s atomic mass
Multiply the mass by the subscript in the chemical formula, if there is one
If it’s a compound and there are multiple elements, add up each element’s molar mass
Example for H₂O:
Hydrogen: H has a subscript of 2 and H has an atomic mass of 1.01 on the periodic table → molar mass = 1.01 x 2 = 2.02
Oxygen: O doesn’t have a subscript O has an atomic mass of 16.00 on the periodic table → molar mass = 16.00 x 1 = 16.00
Total: 2.02 + 16.00 = 18.02
H₂O has a molar mass of 18.02 grams/mole
How to Solve Questions
Steps:
Find atomic masses on the periodic table
Multiply by subscripts
Add totals to find the molar mass of the compound

Topic: Gas Laws
Questions: 15, 64
Gas particles move constantly and collide with the walls of their container or other particles, creating pressure. This is because gas particles have high average kinetic energy and are constantly moving at high speed, which causes frequent collisions as the particles buzz around in the limited space of their container.
When volume decreases, gas particles have less space, so they collide more often.
That means:
lower volume → higher pressure
higher volume → lower pressure
This relationship is called Boyle’s Law.
PV = nRT
Pressure x Volume = moles of gas x gas constant* x temperature
*The gas constant is a constant number that states the proportionality between the amount of a substance and its temperature. 
How to Solve Questions
Remember:
Pressure and volume are inversely related
As volume decreases, pressure increases and vice versa.



Topic: Reaction Types
Questions: 16, 26, 47, 50, 53, 55
Chemical reactions describe how substances change into new substances. Different reaction types follow recognizable patterns depending on what happens to the reactants.
Synthesis Reaction
Two or more substances combine to form one product.
General pattern:
A + B → AB
Decomposition Reaction
One compound breaks apart into simpler substances.
General pattern:
AB → A + B
Single Replacement Reaction
One element replaces another element in a compound.
General pattern:
A + BC → AC + B
Double Replacement Reaction
Two compounds exchange ions with each other.
General pattern:
AB + CD → AD + CB
Combustion Reaction
A substance reacts with oxygen and releases energy, usually in the form of heat and light.
How to Solve Questions
AB + CD → AD + CB → double replacement
One compound breaking apart → decomposition
Reaction with oxygen releasing energy → combustion
Two substances joining together → synthesis

Topic: Periodic Trends
Questions: 18, 39, 43, 58, 59
Periodic trends are patterns that occur across the periodic table. These trends help predict how elements behave chemically.
Electronegativity:
Electronegativity measures how strongly an atom attracts electrons in a bond. Electronegativity generally increases from left to right across the periodic table because atoms have more protons pulling on electrons.
*Fluorine has the highest electronegativity of all elements.
Metalloids:
Metalloids have properties between metals and nonmetals. They may conduct electricity somewhat, but not as well as metals (e.x, silicon).
Elements in the same group often have similar chemical properties because they have the same number of valence electrons.
How to Solve Questions
Highest electronegativity → fluorine
Trend increasing left to right → electronegativity
Element with mixed metal and nonmetal properties → metalloid
Same group → similar chemical properties

Topic: Intermolecular Forces
Questions: 27
Intermolecular forces (IMFs) are forces of attraction between molecules. These forces are NOT actual chemical bonds like ionic or covalent bonds. Instead, they are weaker attractions that affect how molecules behave near each other.
Intermolecular forces help explain many physical properties of substances, including: boiling point, melting point, surface tension, etc.
In general:
Stronger intermolecular forces → particles stick together more strongly
Stronger IMFs = higher boiling points, higher melting points, and slower evaporation
Weaker intermolecular forces → particles separate more easily
Weaker IMFs = lower boiling points, faster evaporation
There are several major types of intermolecular forces:
London Dispersion Forces
These are the weakest intermolecular forces and occur in ALL molecules. They happen because electrons move around constantly, creating temporary uneven charges.
Dipole-Dipole Forces
These occur in polar molecules, where one side of the molecule is slightly positive and the other side is slightly negative. Opposite charges attract each other.
Hydrogen Bonding
This is a particularly strong type of dipole-dipole force. Hydrogen bonding occurs when hydrogen is bonded to: Fluorine, Oxygen, or Nitrogen (FON molecules)
Hydrogen bonding has key properties of having a high boiling point, high surface tension, and an ability to dissolve many substances
Water molecules have hydrogen bonding, which is why water molecules “stick together” so well.
How to Solve Questions
 First, determine whether the molecules are polar or nonpolar. Polar molecules have uneven charge distribution, while nonpolar molecules have more even charge distribution.
Then identify the strongest intermolecular force present:
 Nonpolar molecules → usually London dispersion forces
 Polar molecules → usually dipole-dipole forces
 Hydrogen bonded to nitrogen, oxygen, or fluorine → hydrogen bonding


 
Topic: Endothermic & Exothermic Reactions
Questions: 28
Chemical reactions either absorb or release energy.
Endothermic Reactions – Absorb energy from the surroundings to facilitate the reaction
This usually occurs when the reactants need energy to carry out the reaction to completion and transform into products.
Exothermic Reactions – Release energy into the surroundings
This occurs when the rearrangement of bonds in a chemical reaction releases energy (usually in the form of heat) as a product.
Endothermic reactions often feel cold because they take in energy, while exothermic reactions feel warmer when the reaction is complete.
How to Solve Questions
Absorbs energy → endothermic
Releases energy → exothermic



Topic: Valence Electrons
Questions: 29, 30, 44
Electrons are arranged in energy levels, also called shells, which are arranged in increasing order from left to right on the periodic table.
Valence electrons are the electrons in the outermost shell of an atom. These electrons are responsible for bonding and chemical reactions.
An atom always wants to have its outermost electron shell full. Any extra electrons that hinder this goal are called valence electrons. 
The atom either wants to give away these electrons to drop down to its next full outer shell or gain more electrons to complete its current outermost shell. 
Example:
Sodium (Na) has 11 electrons, so the arrangement is: 2 - 8 - 1
This means:
2 electrons in first shell
8 in second shell
1 in third shell ← the third shell can normally hold up to 18 electrons, but it only has 1 right now, so it’s an incomplete shell
How to Solve Questions
To find the electron configuration:
Find the element’s group using the periodic table
Count total electrons
Remember the maximum that each shell can hold
1st → 2
2nd → 8
3rd → 8
4th → 32
Fill electron shells in order:
first shell → 2
second shell → 8
third shell → continue filling



Topic: Molecular Geometry & Polarity
Questions: 31, 45, 49, 62, 65
Molecular geometry describes the 3D shape of molecules based on how electron pairs repel each other.
This shape is determined by the number of lone pairs of electrons and tells you the angle that is formed between central bonds.
Molecular shape affects the polarity of a compound. For example, water is polar because its bent shape causes uneven charge distribution.
How to Solve Questions
To do molecular geometry questions, a VESPR chart is needed. Match the number of lone pairs and the steric number of your element to the chart’s columns and rows to identify the shape.
